# Evidence control / 证据控制

Use this reference when the document contains claims that could be disputed, dated, measured, attributed, or acted on.

## Build a private evidence ledger

Before drafting, classify important statements in a working ledger. A usable ledger records the exact claim, its class, source title or origin, page/section/link or other locator, source date or version, access date when relevant, and the conclusion it supports.

| Class | Meaning | Safe wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplied statement | Explicitly provided by the user or source material but not independently verified | Attribute it to the source; use `[待核]` when its truth materially affects the decision. |
| Authoritative record | Stated in an approved or controlling source within its valid scope | State within that scope and retain the exact locator and version. |
| Verified fact | Confirmed against a current, authoritative source | State directly with a nearby citation when the format permits. |
| Inference | Reasoned conclusion from facts | Use `据此判断`、`表明`、`可能意味着` and show the basis. |
| Recommendation | Proposed action or preference | Use `建议`、`可考虑`、`优先` and explain the tradeoff. |
| Unknown | Material information is absent or conflicting | Mark `[待补]` or `[待核]`; do not guess. |

Do not relabel a user assertion as a fact merely because it appears in the brief. Ask whether the source proves the underlying claim or only proves that someone made the statement.

The full ledger can remain a drafting aid, but traceability cannot disappear at delivery. For reports and plans containing disputed, current, quantitative, or externally attributed claims, include nearby citations or a compact source appendix by default. If the requested format forbids an appendix, retain exact locators in the working material and surface any claim that still requires verification.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. authoritative documents supplied for the task;
2. current primary sources such as laws, policies, standards, official statistics, contracts, and original research;
3. reliable secondary analysis with clear attribution;
4. stakeholder statements and media reports, identified as statements rather than established fact.

Search snippets, unsourced summaries, and model recollection are discovery aids, not final evidence.

## Current and sensitive claims

Verify information that can change, including office holders, organizational structures, product capabilities, prices, schedules, laws, standards, and statistics. Record the effective or access date when it matters.

For confidential material, minimize reproduction. Do not move private sources into public tools or outputs unless the user has authorized that destination.

## Quotations and numbers

- Quote only when the exact wording matters; otherwise paraphrase faithfully.
- Keep units, time periods, denominators, and comparison baselines attached to numbers.
- Do not turn a target into a result, a sample into a population, or correlation into causation.
- If two sources conflict, show the conflict or use the more authoritative source and explain why.

## Verification pass

Before delivery, challenge every sentence containing a number, superlative, named authority, policy requirement, causal claim, or external attribution. It must be sourced, qualified, or removed.
