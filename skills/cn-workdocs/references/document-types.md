# Document types / 文种路由

Choose the smallest structure that serves the reader. The patterns below are defaults, not mandatory headings.

## Research or analysis report / 调研分析报告

Best for answering a question with evidence and recommending a decision.

Recommended logic:

1. executive judgment and scope;
2. current state and verified evidence;
3. causes, constraints, and alternative explanations;
4. options with benefits, costs, risks, and dependencies;
5. recommendation, implementation conditions, and stop criteria;
6. sources, assumptions, and items requiring verification.

Avoid turning a research report into an encyclopedia. Organize evidence around the decision question.

## Proposal or implementation plan / 方案与实施计划

Best for aligning people around an intended outcome and execution path.

Recommended logic:

1. problem and necessity;
2. objective and measurable boundary;
3. scope, architecture, or work packages;
4. phased actions with owner, output, milestone, and acceptance criterion;
5. resources and governance;
6. risks, dependencies, rollback or stop conditions.

Distinguish objectives from activities. `建设平台` is an activity; the operational outcome and acceptance criterion must be explicit.

## Work report or summary / 工作报告与总结

Best for explaining what changed, why it matters, and what follows.

Recommended logic:

1. overall result and key judgment;
2. completed work with evidence of effect;
3. unresolved problems and causes;
4. next actions, owners, and timing.

Do not equate meetings held, documents issued, or systems launched with outcomes unless evidence shows the effect.

## Meeting minutes / 会议纪要

Best for creating a shared, actionable record.

Separate:

- decisions reached;
- actions with owner and deadline;
- open questions and escalation points;
- background discussion needed to interpret the decision.

Do not manufacture consensus. Attribute unresolved positions when necessary and avoid recording irrelevant conversational detail.

## Leadership speech / 领导讲话

Best for aligning judgment, responsibility, and action through spoken language.

Recommended arc:

1. why the moment matters;
2. a small number of memorable judgments;
3. responsibilities and concrete action;
4. risks or discipline that must be held;
5. concise closing mobilization.

Write for the ear: shorter sentences, controlled repetition, clear transitions, and one main idea per paragraph. Estimate speaking time and revise after reading aloud. Do not fill length with parallel slogans.

## Mixed or unclear request

Choose a primary document type and borrow only the modules that support its purpose. State the assumed type when choosing incorrectly would materially change the result.
