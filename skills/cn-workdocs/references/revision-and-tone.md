# Revision and tone / 修改与语气

Use this reference when improving an existing draft or reducing generic AI language.

## Preserve before changing

Identify protected content:

- approved facts, numbers, names, and dates;
- authoritative quotations or policy wording;
- assigned responsibilities and commitments;
- the author's actual judgment and intended audience;
- required headings, template constraints, and length.

If the draft is internally inconsistent, flag the conflict instead of silently choosing a version.

## Three-pass revision

### 1. Logic pass

Check whether conclusions follow from evidence, sections answer the stated problem, options are compared fairly, and proposed actions address the identified causes.

### 2. Density pass

Remove repeated scene-setting, synonymous slogans, empty intensifiers, and paragraphs that merely announce importance. Preserve useful context and necessary institutional framing.

Replace vague expressions when the source material permits:

| Weak expression | Stronger direction |
| --- | --- |
| 加强协同 | Name the parties, mechanism, cadence, and decision right. |
| 持续推进 | State the next milestone, owner, and due condition. |
| 取得明显成效 | Give observable evidence or qualify the claim. |
| 进一步提升 | Name the baseline, target, or specific capability. |
| 形成合力 | Explain how conflicts and handoffs will be resolved. |

### 3. Voice pass

Use formal modern Chinese, not translated English syntax. Vary sentence length, keep modifiers close to the noun they qualify, and prefer verbs over abstract noun stacks. Retain deliberate rhetorical rhythm in speeches, but keep reports and plans direct.

## Anti-slop audit

Search the draft for:

- openings that could introduce any organization or topic;
- claims of importance without consequences;
- three-part slogans whose items overlap;
- repeated `通过……实现……` constructions;
- invented certainty where the evidence is incomplete;
- actions without an owner, object, timing, output, or acceptance test;
- conclusions that merely repeat section headings.

Revise only where the change increases accuracy, clarity, decision value, or speakability. Do not erase the author's voice in pursuit of uniform style.
